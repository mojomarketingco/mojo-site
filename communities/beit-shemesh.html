Beit ShemeshJerusalemThe Centre

Beit Shemesh is not one place, and that catches people out. Thirty minutes from Jerusalem, forty-five from Tel Aviv, built across a set of hills, and every hill has its own character. People here tell you which neighborhood they live in, not which city.

English speakers started arriving in Sheinfeld in 1991. Three decades on, in parts of Ramat Beit Shemesh you will hear as much English as Hebrew walking to shul.

A note on how this is written. These descriptions name the communities and institutions that exist in each area. They are not a guide to who belongs where.

The neighborhoods

Every area hereis a different decision.

Ramat Beit Shemesh Aleph

The oldest of the Ramah neighborhoods and the one with everything already built. Four supermarkets, a commercial strip you can walk to, English-speaking doctors, and the biggest park in the city. Religiously the most mixed part of Beit Shemesh — Dati Leumi, Haredi and Hassidic communities, Ashkenazi and Sephardi, often on the same street. Around 25,000 people.

Ramat Shilo

Built from 2007 on the slope below Aleph. Formally part of it, but it has its own feel: newer, quieter, and the planting has had time to grow in. You look out at hills rather than at the building opposite.

Mishkafayim and M3

The newest building on the lower slopes. Bigger apartments, better finishes, and views across the valley. The more expensive corner of the Ramah, and people pay it for space rather than for status.

Ramat Beit Shemesh Gimmel

Where most of the growth is. Gimmel 1, officially Kiryat Avi Ezri, is several thousand homes at lower density than Aleph, so apartments are larger with more open ground between them. Strongly Yeshivish and Haredi, with a substantial Anglo community from the States, the UK and South Africa.

Neve Shamir

The newest neighborhood, planned for around 5,000 families, looking out over the Ella valley. Everything is new construction — lifts, parking, a mamad and a balcony as standard. If what you get for the money matters most, look here first.

Sheinfeld

Down the hill and older than the Ramah, and where the Anglo community here began. Cottages and semi-attached houses rather than apartment blocks, gardens, quiet streets, and a strong centrist and Modern Orthodox community.

Worth knowing

The partsnobody advertises.

It is genuinely built on hills. Getting between neighborhoods often means driving, and walking means climbing. Something is always under construction somewhere, and has been for thirty years. The newer neighborhoods are still waiting on some of their shops and services. And the character changes street to street — visit on a Shabbat before you decide.

Selling here

What movesa property in Beit Shemesh.

Buyers here pick a neighborhood before they pick an apartment, and often a school and a shul before either. If a listing does not make its exact location and community obvious, buyers will not do that work themselves.

Most of the demand never touches a property portal. It moves through the neighborhood WhatsApp groups and the shul lists. That is where we put your property.
