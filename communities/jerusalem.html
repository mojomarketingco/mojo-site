Beit ShemeshJerusalemThe Centre

The Anglo neighborhoods sit mostly in the south and centre, and they are old. Stone, arches, gardens, buildings that were here a century before the state was. Baka, the German Colony and Old Katamon are all within walking distance of each other, which is rare in Israel and is a large part of why people move here.

In parts of the south, roughly a third of your neighbors will be English speakers.

A note on how this is written. These descriptions name the communities and institutions that exist in each area. They are not a guide to who belongs where.

The neighborhoods

Every area hereis a different decision.

Baka

Feels like a village the city grew around. Narrow streets, old stone houses, gardens, jasmine in spring. Quieter than the German Colony next door and noticeably cheaper for the same space. Sephardi families who have been here for generations, Anglo families who came for the German Colony and stayed, and a steady arrival of young couples.

The German Colony

Busier and more commercial, built around Emek Refaim, which is as close as Anglo Jerusalem gets to a high street. Beautiful Templer and Arab-era stone, and the highest prices in the south. You are paying for the address and for being able to walk to everything.

Old Katamon

Where a lot of families end up. Good schools, good parks, cafes without the Emek Refaim crowds, and a large English-speaking community. Religiously mixed and comfortable with it.

Rechavia and Shaarei Chesed

Central, established, and quietly expensive. 1930s architecture, mature trees, and a walk to the city centre. Long favoured by academics, professionals and Anglo families who arrived decades ago.

Arnona

High up, around 800 metres, with views east toward the desert. It has become the answer for people who want to be near Baka and the German Colony without those prices. Mostly 1990s and 2000s apartments, so lifts and parking are far more common.

Ramat Eshkol

North of the centre, and materially cheaper than the south for the same number of rooms. One of the most sought-after addresses for young Orthodox families, with a long-established Anglo community. It sits on the light rail with a free park and ride.

Worth knowing

The partsnobody advertises.

It is the most expensive market in the country outside central Tel Aviv, and the gap between neighborhoods is wide. Parking in Baka and the German Colony is genuinely difficult and some streets have none at all. Many of the beautiful old buildings have no lift and no step-free entrance, which matters more than people expect.

Selling here

What movesa property in Jerusalem.

Buyers here are buying a street as much as an apartment. Light, outdoor space, a lift and a parking spot move a Jerusalem price more than square metres do.

A large share of buyers are overseas and will have a shortlist before they land. The photography and the video are often the only viewing that happens before an offer, which makes them decisive rather than helpful.

Every week on the market at the wrong price costs more than this entire campaign.
